# Terminological terms, concepts, carriers, and formal terms

The repository had two active `term.md` carriers with identical prose. Both
defined the mathematical object generated by a formal grammar, even though one
sat at information science and the malformed term-science subtree contained
software for glossary records. The resulting contract used one word for a
designation, a formal expression, and a persisted record. That is the mismatch
the engineering index exposed: legacy glossary metadata was being judged by an
implementation whose domain meaning had never been stated unambiguously
upstream.

## The terminological chain

The chain needed by glossary work is:

1. A **referent** is something under discussion.
2. A **concept** is the unit of knowledge through which the community
   distinguishes what is under discussion, including relevant
   characteristics.
3. A **designation** is an expression used to communicate that concept.
4. A **term** is a designation admitted for a concept in a bounded domain and
   community.
5. A **term record** documents that association and its warrant.
6. A **carrier** is the material or digital form in which the record persists.

Dahlberg is the load-bearing source for keeping referent, characteristics, and
designation distinct [@dahlberg1978]. The repository-specific schema adds no
new identity claim: it merely says which pieces a term record must expose so
software and readers can handle the distinction without guessing.

Names and aliases are designations. An alias is a second admitted designation
for the same concept in the same scope; it is not evidence that the two strings
are globally synonymous. Keywords are retrieval or composition keys. Relations
and predicates are semantic assertions connecting identified things. A record
may contain aliases, keywords, and relations, but none of those fields is the
term's identity simply by appearing in its carrier.

## What FCA contributes

Formal concept analysis begins from a formal context of objects, attributes,
and incidence, then derives closed extent–intent pairs [@ganter-wille1999]. The
repository's active FCA note documents exactly that Galois-closure boundary.
FCA can therefore reveal stable attribute groupings and a concept lattice that
inform concept analysis. It cannot, without additional community and language
evidence, decide that two designations are aliases or choose the preferred term
for a concept.

The word *concept* in *formal concept* names a mathematical closed pair. A
terminological concept is a unit of knowledge in a discourse community. The
two may be related by an analysis, but they are not definitionally identical.

## What lambda calculus and CHL contribute

A formal term is generated by a grammar. Lambda terms, for example, are built
from variables, abstraction, and application, with substitution and reduction
governed by the calculus [@barendregt1984]. Curry–Howard–Lambek relates typed
terms, proofs, and morphisms. This supplies powerful structure for expressions
inside formal systems, but it does not make a glossary designation into a
lambda term or a Markdown term record into a proof.

The right dependency is therefore one-way and qualified: glossary science may
use formal languages to specify record constraints and may use FCA to analyze
conceptual structure. Neither mathematical theory defines what a community's
terminological term is.

## Record consequences

The science schema requires explicit carrier identity, name, and body. It keeps
aliases and keywords separate, holds semantic assertions under `relations`,
published evidence under `references`, and dates and source custody under
`provenance`. That division prevents citations and dates from being mistaken
for domain relations and prevents a filename, display name, or alias from
silently substituting for record identity.

The executable term-engineering service is the receiver of this contract. Its
follow-on work is to reconcile its admitted Markdown and in-memory record shape
with the science schema, then assay legacy glossary carriers by migration rather
than treating every old frontmatter key as a semantic relation.
